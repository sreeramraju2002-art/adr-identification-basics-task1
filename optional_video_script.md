# Optional 2–3 Minute Walkthrough Script

Hello, this is my Task 1 learning project on ADR Identification Basics. The case I am presenting is entirely fictional and for educational purposes only.

In this scenario, a fictional 34-year-old adult starts amoxicillin for a presumed bacterial throat infection. On the third day of treatment, an itchy red rash on the trunk and arms is reported.

Amoxicillin is identified as the suspected medicine because the reaction was reported after treatment began. However, a timeline alone does not prove causality. Other explanations may exist, and important details such as exact dose, medical and allergy history, concomitant medicines, clinical assessment, and outcome are missing.

The seriousness of the event cannot be formally determined from the limited information. The scenario does not report breathing difficulty or facial swelling, but missing information should not be treated as proof that all serious features are absent.

For a complete assessment, I would follow up for the exact onset and progression of the rash, all medicines and recent exposures, prior antibiotic exposure, management, and outcome. I would document the available information clearly and distinguish facts from unknowns.

The key learning is that pharmacovigilance requires structured documentation, careful review of medication timelines, and an evidence-based assessment without claiming more certainty than the case supports. Thank you.
